# TASK-1014 — Fault, Failure & Degraded-Quality Simulation

Status: **DONE** (2026-10-12)
Linear: [ZFA-69](https://linear.app/recruit-verse/issue/ZFA-69)

Phase: **10 — Agent-Ready Engineering & Full Catalog Go Simulation**

## Objective
Standardize device fault and degraded-state behavior across the catalog simulator.

## Examples
Communication loss, bad quality, stuck sensor/value, overload, overcurrent, overtemperature, drive trip, motor fault, dry run, stuck valve/actuator, limit-switch disagreement and controller/device unavailable.

## Acceptance criteria
- Fault injection, clear and reset behavior are explicit and testable.
- Latching behavior is model-driven.
- Faults propagate through feedback/quality without bypassing PLC logic.

## Prerequisites
- TASK-1010 (DONE): every fault of every profile stated in the contract with its reset rule, effect and quality; `badQualityOnFaults`.
- TASK-1011 (DONE): model-raised faults (`Context.Raise` / `Release`), quality from any observer.
- TASK-1013 (DONE): served devices and their register maps, which carry the protocol's fault.

## Decisions (agent, 2026-10-12; for the owner to confirm)
- **One standard, written down, the same in both engines.** `docs/device-simulation.md` → Faults, failures and degraded quality states what injecting, clearing, raising, latching, silence and quality mean; `internal/device/faults.go` repeats it beside the code. Nothing of the existing fault handling changed meaning; the standard names what was there and closes the gaps the examples named.
- **Two generic faults, declared once.** `OFFLINE` (device unavailable) is appended to every profile's fault list by the runtime's `Describe` and by the contract's profile index, so the two cannot disagree and no profile file lists it; `COMM_LOSS` is the fault of every register map, gained by a device for being served. Both are silent, non-latching and bad-quality: an unpowered or unreachable device reports nothing.
- **Unavailable means de-energized and wiped.** An `OFFLINE` device is not stepped, every output reads false / 0 as powered-down wiring reads, and its physics is wiped at once (a drive's frequency, a relay's contacts), so what it feeds or switches reads it as gone without any peer logic changing; when the fault ends it restarts from its initial state, untripped, as equipment does after a power cycle. Commands keep landing meanwhile and do nothing.
- **Quality travels along `measures` links.** A device's quality is BAD while a bad-quality fault, unavailability, loss of communication or a runtime quarantine is present, and while the device it measures is BAD; every change is a `QUALITY_CHANGED` event, and the bridge carries the quality to the feedback tags as before. Faults act on feedback only; nothing writes a PLC output.
- **Limit-switch disagreement is a silent fault of the valve and the cylinder** (`LIMIT_SWITCH_FAULT`, `END_SENSOR_FAULT`): both switches read at once, the device reports nothing, the PLC's logic detects it. Quality stays GOOD: the value is real, the switches are wrong.
- **The catalog lists `OFFLINE` on every simulated model**, so the simulator screen can inject it, and a vendor model lists it without a source: it is Z-Factory's generic fault, not a vendor's claim (`GENERIC_FAULT_CODES`). `COMM_LOSS` is accepted only on a served model.

## Acceptance
- Fault injection, clear and reset behavior are explicit and testable: the standard is written in one place and every fault of every profile states its reset rule, effect and quality (checked by the contract test); injecting, clearing, model-raising and resetting are each one operation with one refusal rule, covered for every kind of fault in both engines, including the new `OFFLINE`, `COMM_LOSS` and disagreement faults, with the parity test holding the two engines to the same outputs and qualities through an outage and a return.
- Latching behavior is model-driven: whether a fault latches is the profile's statement (the catalog may override it per model, never the UI), a trip ends only by the reset signal, the operator or a power cycle, and the motor starter's self-raised overload latches by the same rule as an injected one.
- Faults propagate through feedback/quality without bypassing PLC logic: an unavailable drive's motor coasts and its pump stops because the drive's feedback and supply read as gone, a transmitter on it reads BAD, the bridge carries the quality to the tags, and no code path writes a PLC output; commands landing on an unavailable device do nothing until it is back.

## Remaining limitations
- `OFFLINE` wipes a device's physics at once; a device with its own energy store (a heater's temperature, a cylinder's position) is reset to its initial state too, which is a simplification stated in the contract's effect.
- A device set aside by the runtime (`FAULTED`, `QUARANTINED`) still reads its frozen outputs, as before; only `OFFLINE` de-energizes.
- Quality travels along `measures` links only; a motor fed by an unavailable drive stays GOOD (it is powered down, not misreporting).
